# module-archive

The archive: people and their family tree, locations and tracks, devices, photos, videos and documents, and finding things among them.

An [EiN](https://github.com/einhq) module. It is developed and tested inside a checkout of EiN core,
as `modules/archive`, and a deployment chooses whether to run it. `mod.json` is its manifest: the core it
needs (`coreApi`), the modules it depends on, and what it exports.

## What it holds

One module for everything the archive holds, in areas that keep their own folders (`lib/<area>/`, `types/<area>/`, `test/<area>/`, `ui/<area>/`) and aliases (`@archive/<area>/…`); people, location, device, media, document and staging were modules of their own until ein/core#442. **Finding things**, at the root (`search` and `facets` are core's since ein/core#442, with archive's `search-*` commands as its text providers): the global `timeline` and `timeline-facets`, the home page's providers `news-albums` (the albums people made lately), `events-anniversaries` (what comes round around a date) and `onthisday-media` (the years with pictures from a calendar day), `find-within` for the map. The search box's reading of words is archive's too (`archive-parse`, `lib/search/`, set as `CFG_SEARCH_PARSER`), and so is what it offers the assistant, in its `etc/assistant.d/`: its commands as tools, what it says of the archive, the family tree (`/family`) and map (`/map/pins`) cards, suggested questions and `agent-eval`'s questions. **people**: Family tree — persons, relationships, events; `gedcom-import` brings a GEDCOM file's people in, matching each individual to a person or making one (`--match`, `--new` and `--skip` decide for an individual), adds the relations, events, homes (a residence at a location of that name) and notes (as the description) they lack, and keeps the file in `/archive/sources/genealogy/`; a `.ged` file dropped in staging waits in the staging area's `genealogy/` (`gedcom-stage`, and its pipeline), where `/staging/genealogy` draws its tree beside the archive's people it matches (`gedcom-read`, which says who each individual would be), lets its owner choose, and imports it. **media**: EXIF extraction, face detection, fingerprinting, reverse geocoding, picture vectors, transcripts and voices. **document**: Text extraction, OCR, semantic indexing. **location**: Location/location search; placing locations (`location-locate`, media centroid then `geocode-location`), kept up to date by `archive-bridge` and the `location-upkeep` / `location-link-point` pipelines; tracks (a device's recorded route is its child `track`; `track-create/append/read/import` take the device, `track-list` lists the devices that have one, GPX import, and a GPX file dropped in staging is imported into the device it names by `track-stage` (the `track-stage` pipeline) or waits in the staging area's `routes/` for someone to name it on `/staging/routes`, the kit's `NodeBrowser` over that folder with a side panel that imports the selected files into the device chosen and shows what the current one says of itself (`gpx-info`: its name, tracks, creator, points and their time span, and the devices it names), while one uploaded on a device's Track tab goes through /tmp into that device's track (`track-stage --device --name`); live tracking from a phone is `tracker-setup`, which gives the device a track and asks core's `track-token` for the link a tracker posts to, and draws the QR code that sets Traccar Client up (`lib/device/qr.ts`); the file is kept in the device's `routes/`, moved there by `track-stage` and copied by `track-import`; the registry keeps `{points, firstTs, lastTs, bbox}`, which `track-list`, the timeline and its facets read for every track at once (`NodeVFS.trackSummaries`) — whoever appended, a command or a routing key — and `track-read` takes `--bbox`/`--from`/`--to`/`--tolerance` for a map viewport, and `--rejected` (or `--only-rejected`) for the fixes core's track rules rejected as it stored them, which `track-mark` overrules for a time range, narrowed to the fixes near a point (`--near`) or to those marked by hand (`--marked`), rejecting or accepting its fixes by hand; the device's Track tab draws the rejected fixes faintly, and a click picks the fix nearest it to reject, with the fixes within 40 m of it that day, to accept, or to hand back to the rules, alone or with every fix rejected by hand that day; `track-move` moves a time range to another device's track, which a trip recorded on the wrong device uses from the Trips tab); trips (`track-trips` cuts a device's track into trips, legs and stays with the thresholds of its kind, its timezone, its homes and its `tripEdits`, `lib/location/trips.ts`, and keeps them in `<device>/.trips`, read again only from where they are settled; the `track-trips` pipeline runs it on `track.changed`, and `trip-list` and `trip-edit` read and correct them on the device's Trips tab; its second step, `track-places`, puts each stay at its home, at the location within 150 m, or at a new location named after the harbour, island, bay or town there, from Overpass and then Nominatim, `lib/location/place-name.ts`). **sources**: The files nodes were read from (`lib/sources/`): a source is kept under the node that owns it, or in `/archive/sources/<kind>/` when none does, and every node read from it links it from its `sources/` (`source-list`). **device**: Camera/recorder tracking, timestamp correction. **staging**: Private per-user media inbox in categories, the groups suggested for it (the organizer, its grouping verbs and the read-only `auto-album-cluster` analysis), duplicate review, existing-album suggestions, and promotion into the shared library
