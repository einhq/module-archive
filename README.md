# module-archive

The archive: people and their family tree, locations and the trips devices make, cameras, photos, videos and documents, and finding things among them.

An [EiN](https://github.com/einhq) module. It is developed and tested inside a checkout of EiN core,
as `modules/archive`, and a deployment chooses whether to run it. `mod.json` is its manifest: the core it
needs (`coreApi`), the modules it depends on, and what it exports.

## What it holds

One module for everything the archive holds, in areas that keep their own folders (`lib/<area>/`, `types/<area>/`, `test/<area>/`, `ui/<area>/`) and aliases (`@archive/<area>/…`); people, location, camera, media, document and staging were modules of their own until ein/core#442. **Finding things**, at the root (`search` and `facets` are core's since ein/core#442, with archive's `search-*` commands as its text providers): the global `timeline` and `timeline-facets`, the home page's providers `news-albums` (the albums people made lately), `events-anniversaries` (what comes round around a date) and `onthisday-media` (the years with pictures from a calendar day), `find-within` for the map. The search box's reading of words is archive's too (`archive-parse`, `lib/search/`, set as `CFG_SEARCH_PARSER`), and so is what it offers the assistant, in its `etc/assistant.d/`: its commands as tools, what it says of the archive, the family tree (`/family`) and map (`/map/pins`) cards, suggested questions and `agent-eval`'s questions. **people**: Family tree — persons, relationships, events; `gedcom-import` brings a GEDCOM file's people in, matching each individual to a person or making one (`--match`, `--new` and `--skip` decide for an individual), adds the relations, events, homes (a residence at a location of that name) and notes (as the description) they lack, and keeps the file in `/archive/sources/genealogy/`; a `.ged` file dropped in staging waits in the staging area's `genealogy/` (`gedcom-stage`, and its pipeline), where `/staging/genealogy` draws its tree beside the archive's people it matches (`gedcom-read`, which says who each individual would be), lets its owner choose, and imports it. **media**: EXIF extraction, face detection, fingerprinting, reverse geocoding, picture vectors, transcripts and voices. **document**: Text extraction, OCR, semantic indexing. **location**: Location/location search; placing locations (`location-locate`, media centroid then `geocode-location`), kept up to date by `archive-bridge` and the `location-upkeep` / `location-link-point` pipelines; tracks (a track is a device's `track` endpoint under `/dev`, core's devices, such as the `position` core's `tracker` driver keeps for a phone, a GPS logger or a boat at `/dev/<device>/position`; it is what travels: its attrs hold how (`kind`, else its device's `class` when that is boat, car, bike or foot), the timezone its days are cut in, its `tripEdits` and its description, set by `track-update`, and its children its home bases (`homes/`, `track-home`), its trips (`.trips`) and the GPX files it was read from (`routes/`, linked from its `sources/`); it is named by its device's label; every track command takes a track's path, or a device or a camera linked to one, by its path or by its name under `/dev` or `/archive/cameras` (`trackOf`); a phone's camera links its device as its child softlink `device` (`camera-device`), and the device's track is the camera's; `track-append/read/import` take the track, `track-list` lists the devices' tracks with their name, device and kind, and a GPX file dropped in staging goes to the device it names (by its label, its manufacturer and model or serial number, or the name, make and model or serial number of a camera linked to it): `track-stage` (the `track-stage` pipeline) writes it to the device, which core's `tracker` driver reads into its track as `cat <file> > /dev/<device>` does, or it waits in the staging area's `routes/` for someone to name the device on `/staging/routes`, the kit's `NodeBrowser` over that folder with a side panel that imports the selected files into the track of the device chosen and shows what the current one says of itself (`gpx-info`: its name, tracks, creator, points and their time span, and the tracks of the devices it names), while one uploaded on a device's Trips tab goes through /tmp into its track (`track-stage --track --name`); the file is kept in the track's `routes/`, moved there by `track-stage` and copied by `track-import`; the registry keeps `{points, firstTs, lastTs, bbox}`, which `track-list`, the timeline and its facets read for every track at once (`NodeVFS.trackSummaries`) — whoever appended, a command, a driver or a routing key — and `track-read` takes `--bbox`/`--from`/`--to`/`--tolerance` for a map viewport, and `--rejected` (or `--only-rejected`) for the fixes core's track rules rejected as it stored them, which `track-mark` overrules for a time range, narrowed to the fixes near a point (`--near`) or to those marked by hand (`--marked`), rejecting or accepting its fixes by hand; core's device page draws a device's track, and archive gives it a Trips tab (a `device.tabs` panel, the route `/trips?device=<path>`, shown on a device with a track): its trips, how it travels (kind, timezone, home bases) and the Fixes view, which draws the rejected fixes faintly, and a click picks the fix nearest it to reject, with the fixes within 40 m of it that day, to accept, or to hand back to the rules, alone or with every fix rejected by hand that day; `track-move` moves a time range to another track, which a trip recorded on the wrong device uses from the Trips tab; an album's map draws the tracks of the devices the cameras of its media are linked to and of those added by hand, `album-device-add/-list/-rm`); trips (`track-trips` cuts a track into trips, legs and stays with the thresholds of how it travels, its timezone, its homes — its own, or the homes of the owners of the cameras linked to its device — and its `tripEdits`, `lib/location/trips.ts`, and keeps them in `<track>/.trips`, read again only from where they are settled; the `track-trips` pipeline runs it on `track.changed`, which `archive-bridge` announces for a device's track under `/dev` whose fixes, kind or timezone or whose device's class change or whose `homes/` gains or loses a link, and `trip-list` and `trip-edit` read and correct them on the device's Trips tab, `trip-album` makes a trip an album (its attr `trip`, which names the track by its node id, so a move of the track or its device keeps it, found again through the `trip_track_id` index); its second step, `track-places`, puts each stay at its home, at the location within 150 m, or at a new location named after the harbour, island, bay or town there, from Overpass and then Nominatim, `lib/location/place-name.ts`). **sources**: The files nodes were read from (`lib/sources/`): a source is kept under the node that owns it, or in `/archive/sources/<kind>/` when none does, and every node read from it links it from its `sources/` (`source-list`). **camera**: Cameras and phones, matched to the media they took by make, model and serial number, and timestamp correction; a phone's camera links the device under `/dev` that records where it was (`camera-device`). A device with a camera's stream (a `stream` endpoint, such as the Automation module's `rtsp` driver gives) is watched by the media service for faces, through the `camera-faces` service (`lib/media/camera-faces.ts`): a face's person, or `unknown`, goes on the device's `faces` log at once, voted on by the stored faces as a photo's face is (`matchFace`), and its picture, once it is gone, is filed under the archive camera linked to the device (`<camera>/faces/<day>/<time>-<track>.jpg`, the camera made when there is none), where it is found and assigned like a photo's face, an unknown one to a new unnamed person; and the media service records the camera's largest stream, so a clip from five seconds before a face until ten after it is gone (or around motion too, as the camera's `clips` says, `camera-update --clips face|motion|off`) is filed beside the faces as a video (`<camera>/clips/<day>/<time>.mp4`), which the pipeline takes as any video; `camera-clip <device>` asks for one now, and an hourly sweep removes the clip days older than the camera's `clipDays` (30, `--clip-days`, 0 keeps them). **staging**: Private per-user media inbox in categories, the groups suggested for it (the organizer, its grouping verbs and the read-only `auto-album-cluster` analysis), duplicate review, existing-album suggestions, and promotion into the shared library
